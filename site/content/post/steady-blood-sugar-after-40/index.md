If you've noticed your energy dipping harder in the afternoons, or cravings hitting differently than they used to, you're not imagining it. After 40, insulin sensitivity naturally starts to shift, which means your body doesn't manage blood sugar quite the same way it did in your 20s and 30s.

The good news: you don't need an extreme diet or a total lifestyle overhaul to feel steadier day to day. A few small, sustainable habits go a long way.

## 1. Pair carbs with protein or fat

Eating carbohydrates on their own — a piece of toast, a handful of crackers, a sweet snack — tends to spike blood sugar quickly and drop it just as fast, which is often what triggers that mid-afternoon crash. Pairing carbs with a source of protein or healthy fat (peanut butter on the toast, cheese with the crackers) slows that spike and keeps energy steadier for longer.

## 2. Take a short walk after meals

Even 10 minutes of walking after eating helps your muscles use glucose more efficiently, which can meaningfully blunt post-meal blood sugar spikes. It doesn't need to be a workout — a walk to the mailbox and back counts.

## 3. Don't skip meals to "save up" calories

Skipping meals often backfires: it leads to bigger blood sugar swings later and stronger cravings, which makes consistent eating harder, not easier. Regular, balanced meals tend to keep things steadier than an irregular eating pattern.

## 4. Prioritize protein at breakfast

Starting the day with protein — eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese — instead of a carb-heavy breakfast has been shown to help stabilize blood sugar for the rest of the day. It also tends to keep you fuller for longer, which makes the rest of your food choices easier.

## 5. Watch your sleep, not just your food

Poor sleep directly affects insulin sensitivity — even one short night can make your body less efficient at managing blood sugar the next day. If you're doing everything right with food but still feel off, your sleep routine might be the missing piece.

None of these changes require perfection. Pick one that feels doable this week, and build from there. That's really the whole philosophy behind Wellness After 40: small, realistic habits that you can actually keep up, not a rigid plan you'll abandon in two weeks.
